CIRCUIT THEORY

Delta-Star Transformation

Learn Δ to Y and Y to Δ conversion with diagrams, formulas and memory tricks.

1. What is Delta-Star Transformation?

Delta-Star Transformation is a technique used in electrical circuit analysis to convert a three-terminal Delta (Δ) network into an equivalent Star (Y) network, or vice versa.

It is especially useful when a circuit cannot be simplified directly using ordinary series and parallel resistance rules.

2. Delta (Δ) to Star (Y) Conversion

In Delta to Star conversion, each Star resistance is calculated by multiplying the two adjacent Delta resistances and dividing by the total sum of all three Delta resistances.

Delta Network Diagram

Equivalent Star Network

Conversion Formulas

RA = (RAB × RCA) / (RAB + RBC + RCA)
RB = (RAB × RBC) / (RAB + RBC + RCA)
RC = (RBC × RCA) / (RAB + RBC + RCA)

Memory Trick: Adjacent Product / Total Sum

Numerator: Multiply the two Delta resistances connected to the corresponding node.

Denominator: Add all three Delta resistances.

Short trick: Adjacent × Adjacent ÷ Total.

3. Star (Y) to Delta (Δ) Conversion

In Star to Delta conversion, first calculate the sum of all pairwise products of the three Star resistances. Then divide this sum by the Star resistance opposite to the required Delta side.

Star Network

Equivalent Delta Network

Conversion Formulas

First calculate the common numerator:

S = RA×RB + RB×RC + RC×RA

Then calculate each Delta resistance:

RAB = S / RC
RBC = S / RA
RCA = S / RB

Memory Trick: Sum of Pairwise Products / Opposite Arm

Numerator: Multiply every possible pair of Star resistances and add them.

Denominator: Use the Star resistance opposite to the Delta side being calculated.

Example: To find RAB, divide by RC because RC is opposite to side AB.

4. Both Conversion Tricks at a Glance

Conversion Numerator Denominator
Δ → Y Adjacent product Sum of all Delta resistances
Y → Δ Sum of all pairwise products Opposite Star arm

5. Numerical Example: Star to Delta

Suppose the three Star resistances are:

RA = 2Ω, RB = 3Ω, RC = 6Ω

Step 1: Calculate the common numerator.

S = (2×3) + (3×6) + (6×2)
S = 6 + 18 + 12 = 36

Step 2: Calculate the Delta resistances.

RAB = 36 / 6 = 6Ω
RBC = 36 / 2 = 18Ω
RCA = 36 / 3 = 12Ω

Final Answer: RAB = 6Ω, RBC = 18Ω, RCA = 12Ω
